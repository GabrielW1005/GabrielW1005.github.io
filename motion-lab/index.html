How Motion Lab works

Drive & watch

Hold ← or → to run the motor. Release to coast. Hold ↓ / S to brake. Press Space to pause, R to reset, and A / D to lower or raise motor strength, including while the simulation runs. On touchscreens, hold a drive button with one finger and move the strength slider with another.

Match the shape

There are 14 graph challenges. Follow the shaded time phases and the dashed gold example. Approximate height and timing are okay: the score looks at rising, falling, flat, and curved sections. Choose the required percentage with the Unlock score slider (1–100%; default 65%). A passing match, with an attempt at every phase, unlocks the next challenge. Changing this setting reassesses a finished attempt immediately. Once unlocked, a challenge stays available for the current session. Your chosen percentage is remembered on this device when browser storage is available.

Distance concave up: speed increases. Distance concave down: speed decreases. Speed concave up: acceleration increases. Speed concave down: acceleration decreases. For speed-curvature phases, keep the car speeding up while changing motor strength. Later challenges mix coasting, stops, and changes in concavity.

Ground & jumps

The car is an ideal point-mass cart. On the road, motor acceleration and the component of gravity along the slope add together. When it reaches a gap, or the road curves away too quickly for gravity to maintain contact, it launches with its existing horizontal and vertical velocity.

In the air, horizontal velocity stays constant and vertical velocity changes by −g every second. Motor and brake inputs have no effect in flight. Landing removes velocity into the surface and keeps velocity along the surface, so some energy is lost. If the car hits a pit wall below the landing, the wall removes its horizontal velocity and the car continues falling under gravity. The camera follows it down before the run ends. With gravity set to zero, it drifts for three seconds after impact before the run ends. There is no bounce, air drag, suspension, or wheel rotational inertia. Wheel spin and car orientation are visual only.

Turn off airborne jumps for a cart that always follows the track, including a guide across the adventure gaps. Graph challenges use a flat road so you can concentrate on the graph.

Read the numbers

Distance includes the entire path, including airborne arcs, and never decreases. Speed is the size of the velocity. Horizontal velocity is positive to the right; vertical velocity is positive upward. Acceleration size includes changes in speed and changes in direction. In free flight it equals g. Landing and pit-wall contact are instantaneous idealized impacts; their impulses are not included in that readout.

The distance graph's slope is speed. The area under the speed graph is distance. Meter markers on the road show horizontal position, not distance traveled.

Motion uses SI units and fixed 1/240 s steps. Ground motion uses fourth-order Runge–Kutta; airborne motion uses projectile equations with within-step launch and landing events. A run ends after 120 simulated seconds or after a missed jump has fallen into the pit. Projectile motion reference.
