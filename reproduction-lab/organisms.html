Who reproduces which way?

Sexual reproduction involves two gametes joining. Asexual reproduction makes offspring without gamete fusion. Some species can do both.

OrganismModeHow it works
HumanSexualSperm and egg join inside the body.3
ZebrafishSexualEggs and sperm are released into water, where fertilization can occur.9
New Mexico whiptailAsexualIn this lizard species, unfertilized eggs can develop into offspring. This is parthenogenesis.10
E. coli bacteriumAsexualOne cell copies its DNA and splits by binary fission.11
Freshwater hydraBothA new hydra can grow from a bud, or eggs and sperm can join.12
Strawberry plantBothFlowers can produce seeds after fertilization. A runner can take root and grow a new plant.13

Each of these strategies still uses sexual reproduction. Track who makes the gametes and where the offspring develop.

How flowering plants fit in

Flowering plants use meiosis and fertilization too. The products of meiosis take an extra step before there are gametes.14

  1. Meiosis makes spores

    Cells in anthers and ovules undergo meiosis. Their products are haploid spores.

  2. Spores grow

    By mitosis, spores form tiny gametophytes. Pollen is the male gametophyte. The female gametophyte stays inside the ovule.

  3. Pollination moves pollen

    A pollen grain reaches a flower’s stigma. A pollen tube grows toward an ovule and delivers sperm cells.

  4. Fertilization starts a seed

    A sperm joins an egg. The zygote becomes an embryo, and the ovule develops into a seed.

Asexual route. A strawberry runner grows a new plant without pollen, egg, or fertilization.13

When and how many?

Human testes and ovaries follow different timelines. The numbers below are approximate and vary among individuals.

Human sperm production

~100–300 million a day

Testes begin making sperm at puberty. Production commonly continues into older adulthood and can decline with age. A batch takes about two months to form.4

Human egg-cell supply

~1–2 million at birth

  • Immature egg cells, called oocytes, form before birth. The count peaks at about 6–7 million before birth, and about 300,000–500,000 remain at puberty.
  • Usually one is released per ovulatory cycle, roughly 400–500 over the reproductive years. Most oocytes naturally break down.
  • By natural menopause, around age 51 on average in the U.S., the supply is very low and ovulation stops.5
