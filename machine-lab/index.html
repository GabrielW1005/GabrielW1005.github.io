Physics notes & sources

These are ideal, quasi-static models, not falling-body simulations. Animation speed is chosen for viewing and is not a measured speed. Real machines need extra input work because some energy becomes heat and sound; total energy is still conserved.

The six classical simple machines are lever, inclined plane, wheel and axle, pulley, wedge, and screw. The geared winch is a compound machine; linked syringes demonstrate the same energy tradeoff through hydraulics.

Distances are measured along each force. For vertical lever forces, use vertical rise/fall, not curved arc length. For a rotating handle pushed tangentially, use its circular travel. A balanced stationary seesaw does no work; its slow tilt illustrates the connection between torque balance and energy.

Problem 8 uses equal input-crank and output-drum radii. The gear teeth exert equal and opposite contact forces; the larger gear increases torque. No force gain is assigned to the tooth contact.

THE BIG IDEA

You can trade distance for force.

A machine transfers energy. An ideal machine has no friction, so the work you put in equals the useful work you get out.

F₁d₁ = F₂d₂

1 = input   2 = output
F is force in newtons (N). d is distance along the force, in meters (m).

Lift a 200 N load through 0.30 m.

Direct lift: 200 × 0.30 = 60 J
With a machine: 50 × 1.20 = 60 J

You push with one quarter of the force, through four times the distance.

F₁ = (F₂ × d₂) ÷ d₁
MA = F₂ ÷ F₁ = d₁ ÷ d₂

Why? Divide F₁d₁ = F₂d₂ by F₁d₂. This gives d₁/d₂ = F₂/F₁. If input distance doubles while output distance and load stay fixed, required input force halves.

  • MA > 1: input travels farther; less input force.
  • MA = 1: equal distances and forces; direction may change.
  • MA < 1: output travels farther; more input force.

At fixed work, doubling a force halves the distance over which that force acts. A machine cannot give you both more force and more distance for free.

This is conservation of energy. Work is a transfer of energy, not a separate substance that is conserved. Real machines turn some energy into heat and sound, so useful output work is smaller. The total energy still balances.
