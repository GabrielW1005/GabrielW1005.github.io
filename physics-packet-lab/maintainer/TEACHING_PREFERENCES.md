# Teaching material preferences

Gabriel requests varied values and realistic units in generated math and science materials.

- Avoid repeatedly choosing the same numerical answers, both within one problem type and across different topics.
- Vary whole numbers, decimals, fractions, signs where meaningful, and the scale of measurements while keeping arithmetic appropriate for the students.
- Use a practical variety of units that fits the situation (for example m/cm/km, s/min/h, m/s/km/h, kg/g, and N/kN). Do not switch units merely to make the wording complicated.
- Show necessary unit conversions clearly in worked examples and keep simulation calculations internally consistent.
- Practice variants must keep their exact measurements, answer units, explanations, and saved responses synchronized between the packet and simulation.
- Preserve published packet originals unless an edit to those originals is explicitly requested.

These preferences were explicitly requested on October 25, 2026 and apply to future updates to this project.

October 25, 2026 extension requirements:
- Keep the handed-out 32-problem paper packet unchanged; add new numerical problems after it in both online views.
- New extension questions should require numerical answers. Rotate unknowns within appropriate practice families: F = ma, m = F/a, a = F/m; p = mv, m = p/v, v = p/m.
- Include equation warm-ups that explicitly show applying division to both sides.
- Keep large Go To Simulation / Go To Packet links at the top of both views, in the same tab.
- Start new experiments with a sensible incorrect guess, preserve student edits, and clamp physical inputs to appropriate finite ranges. Negative values remain allowed when they represent direction.
- Provide autosaving, portable downloadable backups, and a readable progress report students can send to their teacher.
- Use clear third-law contact and buoyancy visuals, without introducing advanced deformation or astrophysics prerequisites.

Handout solution policy (October 25, 2026):
- Original printed questions and printed arithmetic warm-ups provide explanations and hints without final answers or worked solutions. Keep answer checking and student progress.
- More Practice variants, changed custom measurements, and online-only extensions may show full step-by-step solutions.
- Hide explicit reference answers and exact answer differences in original-problem simulation feedback. Keep simulations and graphs available as evidence students can interpret.

- Accept decimals, negative numbers using `-`, and fractions using `/` in answer fields and numerical experiment controls; keep valid fraction answers intact when saving.

Numerical-answer preferences (October 27, 2026):
- Choose varied, nonrepetitive numerical answers within and across topics; use a meaningful mix of whole numbers, decimals, fractions, and signs while keeping values realistic and arithmetic grade-appropriate.
- Never choose 1 or -1 as the final answer to a generated calculation problem. Avoid answers of 2 or 3 wherever practical; do not make the exercise harder merely by using awkward or unrealistic measurements.
- Check the full set of calculated answers before finalizing a worksheet, test, or practice set, and revise repetitive or overly simple results.
- These rules apply to numerical calculation answers, not conceptual relationships such as acceleration doubling or being halved.

Test formatting preference (October 28, 2026):
- Never print a time limit, test duration, or allotted completion time on tests. Timing may guide planning, but must not appear on the student test.
