Why cells divide

Repair & replacement

Some living skin cells divide to make new cells. New cells grow and move to help repair a cut and replace cells that are worn out. Dead skin cells do not divide.

Living cells near damaged tissue can divide to help repair it.

Cell division also helps multicellular organisms grow. In some single-celled eukaryotes, division is a way to reproduce.

Review the quiz & study-guide topics

Interphase & structures

  • Interphase: usually the longest phase; growth and preparation.
  • Chromatin: DNA with proteins, loosely packed during interphase.
  • Sister chromatids: two copies in one duplicated chromosome.
  • Centromere: region where sister chromatids are joined; spindle fibers attach through kinetochores.

The four mitosis stages

  • Prophase: chromosomes condense.
  • Metaphase: duplicated chromosomes line up in the middle.
  • Anaphase: sister chromatids separate toward opposite poles.
  • Telophase: nuclear membranes form around the two sets.

Division & its results

  • Cytokinesis: cytoplasm divides.
  • Plant cells: a cell plate grows from the center outward.
  • Daughter cells: same chromosome number as the parent.
  • Skin repair: living cells divide to replace lost or damaged cells.
